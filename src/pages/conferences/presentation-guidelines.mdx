## Competitive Paper Presentations

### Session Format

<Callout kicker="75-minute session">

**Competitive sessions are 75 minutes** with typically 4 papers:

| Time | Activity |
| :--- | :--- |
| 2 min | Session chair introduces theme and papers |
| 60 min | Four 15-minute presentations |
| 13 min | Audience Q&A |

**Your presentation time is 15 minutes.** Session chairs will enforce time limits to ensure fair treatment of all presenters and adequate discussion time.

</Callout>

### Before Your Session

Arrive 10 minutes early to:

- Test equipment and load your presentation
- Introduce yourself to the chair and co-presenters
- Confirm presentation order
- Distribute any handouts

> *If you cannot attend, notify your session chair immediately.*

### Technical Requirements

| Category | Requirement |
| :--- | :--- |
| **File Format** | PowerPoint (.pptx) or PDF. Bring on USB drive. Email yourself a backup. |
| **Fonts** | Arial or Times New Roman only. Minimum 24-point size. |
| **Colors** | High contrast for readability. Avoid red-green combinations. |
| **Equipment** | Projector and computer provided. Confirm special needs in advance. |

### Presentation Best Practices

#### Structure Your Talk

<Callout kicker="Structure">

**Start strong:**

- Lead with your main finding or contribution
- Use a real-world example to illustrate the problem
- State clearly what is **new** about your work

**For each section:**

- **Theory:** What is the problem? Why does it matter? What do you add?
- **Methods:** Briefly justify your approach and establish face validity
- **Results:** Report direction of effects and what the data tell you—not statistical tests
- **Conclusion:** What have we learned? What should be done next?

</Callout>

#### Handling Q&A

- Listen carefully; repeat questions if needed for the room to hear
- Acknowledge good points even when they challenge your work
- If you don't know an answer, say so and suggest how you would investigate it
- Invite questioners to continue the conversation afterward
- Keep responses concise to allow multiple questions

## Interactive (Poster) Presentations

Interactive sessions provide in-depth, informal feedback on work-in-progress through poster or roundtable format. Senior scholars attend specifically to provide feedback.

### Poster Specifications

| Specification | Requirement |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Maximum size** | A0 (841×1189 mm / 33×47 inches) |
| **Orientation** | Portrait (not landscape) |
| **Font size** | Readable from 1+ meters (body 24+ pt, headings 36+ pt) |
| **Language** | English only |
| **Required elements** | Title, authors, affiliations, contact email for lead author |

### During Your Poster Session

- **Prepare to facilitate discussion:** Bring business cards, paper, sticky notes, and markers
- **Stay at your poster** for the entire session
- **Engage visitors proactively:** *"Would you like me to walk you through it?"*
- **Be ready to discuss** limitations, alternative approaches, and next steps
- **If few people visit:** Use the time to visit other posters, build connections, and observe effective designs

## Accessibility Considerations

Create presentations that work for all attendees:

<Callout tone="muted">

- Use **high-contrast color schemes** (avoid light text on light backgrounds)
- **Describe all visual elements** verbally (charts, graphs, images)
- Speak clearly at a **moderate pace**
- Avoid discipline-specific jargon when possible; define necessary terms
- Provide **handouts** for those who want to follow along

</Callout>

## Troubleshooting

<Callout title='"My presentation won\'t load"' tone="muted">

- This is why you emailed yourself a backup—download from email
- PDF format is more reliable than PowerPoint if compatibility issues arise
- Have your 1-page handout ready; you can present from that if necessary

</Callout>

<Callout title='"I\'m running out of time"' tone="muted">

- Skip to your conclusion; summarize findings briefly
- Offer to discuss details during Q&A or afterward
- **Never speak faster**—it reduces comprehension

</Callout>

<Callout title='"Someone is challenging my work aggressively"' tone="muted">

- Listen fully, then acknowledge the valid elements of their critique
- You do not need to defend every choice in real-time
- *"That is an excellent point I will consider as I revise"* is always appropriate
- Session chairs should redirect hostile questions; make eye contact if you need support

</Callout>

<Callout title='"No one is asking questions"' tone="muted">

- Session chairs typically have prepared questions
- You can prompt: *"One question I have gotten before is..."*
- Silence does not mean your work is not interesting—audiences may be processing

</Callout>

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I distribute handouts?**
Yes. Bring 30-40 copies. A 1-page summary with your key contribution, methods, and contact information is most effective.

**What if my co-author is presenting instead of me?**
Either author can present. Coordinate in advance and ensure the presenter can answer methodology questions.

**Are sessions recorded?**
Check the current year's conference policies. If sessions are recorded, presenters will be notified in advance.

**Can I include video or multimedia?**
Yes, but embed all media files in your presentation and test thoroughly beforehand. Have a static backup slide in case of technical issues.

**What's the dress code?**
Smart casual for all events. Conference rooms can vary in temperature; bring layers.
